# EXP-002 — Policy comparison (pilot scale)

Status: **frozen before any run** (committed before the first EXP-002 call; hash below, recorded in a follow-up commit).
Frozen at commit: d9ab010
Epistemic tag of results: `measured` (recorded model output only)

## Question
At a fixed window budget, which compaction policy leaves an agent most able to act on a planted constraint and a planted identifier, and does behaviour follow what the policy mechanically keeps?

## What is already known without a model
The deterministic survival grid (`results/survival/grid.json`, tagged `deterministic`: real policy code on seeded histories, no model) shows what each non-model policy keeps. With the rule planted at turn 1 (the default) and a budget of 2400 tokens, `recency_truncate` kept it in 0 of 20 histories, `head_tail` in 20 of 20 and `observation_mask` in 20 of 20; at a budget of 1800, `observation_mask` kept it in 0 of 20 because its fallback to dropping history fired (ADR-019). This experiment asks whether agent behaviour follows those survival rates. It does not test survival itself.

## Hypotheses (directional, stated before running)
- **H1** `recency_truncate` violates more than `head_tail`. Why: the first drops the rule and the second keeps it verbatim (grid above). ConstraintRot reports the same ordering on its harness (docs/PRIOR_ART.md).
- **H2** `llm_summary` (prompt v1) violates more than `head_tail`. Why: a generic summary may drop or reword the rule; `head_tail` cannot reword it.
- **H3** `observation_mask` at budget 2400 violates no more than `control` plus noise. Why: it keeps every user turn, so the rule survives verbatim (grid above). This is a prediction that could fail, for example if masked tool outputs confuse the model.
- Where survival is verbatim, I expect violation near the `control` rate; where it is gone, near the `floor` (chance) rate. The interesting results are the exceptions.

## Design
- Packs: `constraint` and `identifier` (ADR-020). Policies: `recency_truncate` (target 0.6), `head_tail` (head ratio 0.3, target 0.6), `observation_mask` (keep 2, target 0.6), `llm_summary` (prompt v1, keep 2 units). Window limit 2400 tokens. Conditions: `floor`, `control`, and per policy `treatment` and its `length_matched` twin.
- Models (filled in 2026-10-21, before freezing): Mistral `ministral-14b-latest` on **both** packs, and NVIDIA `openai/gpt-oss-20b` on the **constraint** pack only. NVIDIA's free trial has a finite credit allowance (about 1,000 credits per forum reports, not verified officially), so its share is limited; this is a budget choice made before seeing any EXP-002 data.
- `llm_summary` settings: `max_summary_tokens=1200` for both models, and for `gpt-oss-20b` also `reasoning_effort: "low"` on the summary call (the configuration preregistered in EXP-001b, so its summaries are not cut off by reasoning). Mistral does not reason first and is sent no reasoning parameter.
- Achieved n is reported per cell; a provider limit that stops a run early is reported as such.
- Seeds: design target 27 per cell; achieved n stated in the report (ADR-018). Per model and pack, the plan is at most 11 calls per seed (2 shared for floor and control, 4 policies x 2 for treatment and length-matched twin, 1 for the summary). `ctxcheck run --dry-run` prints the estimate per policy. Free-tier daily limits decide how many days that takes; `ctxcheck run --dry-run` prints the estimate.
- Temperature: provider default, recorded. Trials: 1 per seed. Only synthetic seeded scenarios are sent.

## Primary comparisons (three, per model and pack, chosen before looking)
1. `treatment(recency_truncate)` vs `treatment(head_tail)`. 2. `treatment(llm_summary)` vs `treatment(head_tail)`. 3. `treatment(observation_mask)` vs `control`. Paired by seed. Benjamini-Hochberg across the three. All other comparisons are exploratory and labelled.

## Analysis plan
As EXP-001: Wilson intervals per cell, exact McNemar, paired bootstrap risk difference, `not_applicable` shown and covered by the sensitivity analysis. Command: `ctxcheck report results/EXP-002`. A policy that ends in its fallback (`observation_mask`) or in `irreducible_budget` is reported with its count, not hidden in the rate.

## Validity checks
Same as EXP-001: `floor` violation below about 50%, `control` violation above about 20%, or more than 20% `not_applicable` makes that model-pack result uninterpretable. In addition: if verbatim survival in the trace disagrees with the deterministic grid for the same policy and seed, the harness has a bug and nothing is reported until it is found.

## Interpretation rules (written in advance)
- A hypothesis is supported for a model if the paired risk difference is in the predicted direction, its 95% bootstrap interval excludes zero and the Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted exact McNemar q is below 0.05 (both, because the bootstrap is optimistic at small n; see EXP-001). Directional with an interval that includes zero is reported as inconclusive. An opposite-direction difference is reported as such.
- Agreement between survival and behaviour is described per policy from the exploratory table; no pooled "survival predicts behaviour" claim is made from a handful of policies.

## What would change our mind / what would make us stop
Change: a validity check fails, or the manual audit finds the summaries rewording the rule in ways the verbatim check misses (then a labelled judge is considered as a secondary measure only). Stop: free-tier caps. No optional stopping.

## Manual audit
Read at least 20 `llm_summary` outputs; record how the rule and the identifier were transformed.

### Validity checks (preregistered)
- Floor (the rule never given) broke it in 26 of 27 for Mistral on the channel rule, 27 of 27 on the account code, and 23 of 26 for gpt-oss-20b: all well above the 50% bar, so a lost rule is visible as a guess.
- Control (full context) broke it in 0 of 27, 0 of 27 and 0 of 27: all below the 20% bar.
- Episodes with no accepted post (the "No summary" column) stay far below the 20% bar in every cell.
- Survival parity (`scripts/survival_parity.py`, `results/EXP-002/survival_parity.json`): for every deterministic-policy episode, the rule's fate in the recorded trace matches the same policy run offline on the same seed, with no mismatch. The harness behaved live as it does offline.

Every model and canary passes, so every result below is interpretable.

### Verdicts (preregistered interpretation rules)
A hypothesis is supported when the paired difference is in the predicted direction, its bootstrap interval excludes zero and q < 0.05. H3 predicts *no* difference, which that rule cannot "support"; it is reported as contradicted or not.

**Mistral `ministral-14b-latest`, channel rule.**
- **H1 supported.** Dropping the oldest turns broke the rule in 25 of 27; keeping head and tail, 0 of 27.
- **H2 inconclusive.** The summary broke it in 3 of 27 against 0 of 27: the predicted direction, but the interval reaches zero and q is above 0.05.
- **H3 not contradicted.** Masking tool output broke it in 0 of 27, as did control (0 of 27).

**Mistral `ministral-14b-latest`, account code.**
- **H1 supported.** 27 of 27 used a wrong code after dropping the oldest turns; 0 of 27 after keeping head and tail.
- **H2 not supported.** The summary: 0 of 27, no different from head and tail.
- **H3 not contradicted.** 0 of 27 against control's 0 of 27.

**NVIDIA `gpt-oss-20b`, channel rule** (summary call with reasoning kept short, as in EXP-001b; no summary was cut off).
- **H1 supported.** Dropping the oldest turns broke the rule in 23 of 27; keeping head and tail, 0 of 27.
- **H2 supported.** The summary broke it in 11 of 25 against 0 of 27 for head and tail. This repeats EXP-001b's finding with a different comparison: here the alternative is a policy that keeps the rule, there it was full context. In 2 of 27 episodes the agent made no post after the summary (the "No summary" column), and the echo check flagged 1 of 27 summaries; the valid-summaries sensitivity analysis in `summary.json` leaves the verdict unchanged.
- **H3 not contradicted, with an exception worth reading.** Masking tool output broke the rule in 2 of 27 against control's 0 of 27; the difference is not significant. In those episodes (seeds 4 and 6) the rule was still in the window, word for word, and the agent posted elsewhere. That is the failure H3's rationale anticipated (masked outputs confusing the model), seen too rarely here to measure.

### Does behaviour follow survival? (exploratory)
Per policy, how often the grade matched what the window still held at the request (kept and followed, or gone and broken): Mistral on the channel rule, drop oldest 25 of 27, head and tail 27 of 27, masking 27 of 27, summary 26 of 27. On the account code: 27 of 27, 27 of 27, 27 of 27, 27 of 27. For gpt-oss-20b: drop oldest 23 of 27, head and tail 27 of 27, masking 25 of 27, summary 23 of 25. The mismatches under dropping the oldest turns are seeds where the rule was gone and the agent picked the right channel anyway: the floor shows a guess sometimes lands.

### What this shows, and what it does not
- With the rule planted near the start, **what a policy keeps decides what the agent does**: the policies that keep the opening turns (head and tail, masking) leave the agent as good as full context; the one that drops them leaves it guessing. That is what the deterministic grid predicted, now confirmed with recorded behaviour on two canaries.
- It is partly true by construction: a rule planted early is exactly what head and tail keeps. A rule planted in the middle of a session, or one stated twice, is a different test (EXP-003 varies the position).
- The summary sits between the two, and depends on the model writing it: Mistral's summaries carried the rule almost every time, gpt-oss-20b's lost it often, as in EXP-001 and EXP-001b.
- Free models, one budget, one prompt version, scripted history, a single compaction. No claim about models in general.
