# EXP-004 — Three facts, one window: which fact does each policy lose? (pilot scale)

Status: **frozen before any run** (this file is committed unchanged before the first EXP-004 call; the hash is recorded in a follow-up commit).
Frozen at commit: 9750518
Epistemic tag of results: `measured` (recorded model output only)

## Question
When one history carries several facts at different depths, does each compaction policy lose a predictable fact, and does the agent's behaviour on each fact follow what that policy kept?

## Why this experiment
EXP-002 planted one rule near the start, so a policy that keeps the opening turns kept it by construction, and "keep head and tail" looked as safe as full context. That conclusion is about the position of the fact, not the policy. This experiment plants three facts at three depths of the same history.

## What is already known without a model
Deterministically (real policy code, seeded histories, no model; `tests/unit/test_multi.py` and the tabulation in the 2026-10-21 notebook entry), at a 2,400-token budget, on all 27 seeds:

| Policy | C1 channel rule (turn 2) | I1 account code (turn 9) | D1 deploy target (turn 16) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `recency_truncate` | gone | gone | kept |
| `head_tail` | kept | gone | gone |
| `observation_mask` | kept | kept | kept |

`llm_summary` is the only policy whose survival is not known in advance: it depends on what the model writes.

## Hypotheses (directional, stated before running)
Behaviour follows survival: where a fact is gone, the agent fails on it at about the floor rate; where it is kept, at about the control rate.
- **H1** `recency_truncate` fails more than `observation_mask` on C1 and on I1, and does not differ from it on D1.
- **H2** `head_tail` fails more than `observation_mask` on I1 and on D1, and does not differ from it on C1.
- **H3** (directional, summary) `llm_summary` fails at least as often as `observation_mask` on every fact. No prediction about which fact a summary keeps; that is reported per fact, exploratory.

## Design
- Packs `multi_channel`, `multi_code`, `multi_deploy` (one shared seeded history with three facts; they differ only in the request, which tests one fact; all three tools are offered in every episode). Window limit 2,400 tokens.
- Policies: `recency_truncate` (target 0.6), `head_tail` (head ratio 0.3, target 0.6), `observation_mask` (keep 2, target 0.6), `llm_summary` (prompt v1, keep 2 units, `max_summary_tokens=1200`). Conditions: `floor`, `control`, and per policy `treatment` with its `length_matched` twin.
- Model: Mistral `ministral-14b-latest` (free tier; ran EXP-002 on both canaries without failures). One model: NVIDIA's trial credits are finite and kept in reserve.
- Seeds 1 to 27; one trial each. Temperature: provider default, recorded. Only synthetic seeded scenarios are sent.
- Every episode records the fate at the probe of all three facts (`fates`), and the grade of the one fact its request tests.

## Primary comparisons (three per pack, chosen before looking)
1. `treatment(recency_truncate)` vs `treatment(observation_mask)`. 2. `treatment(head_tail)` vs `treatment(observation_mask)`. 3. `treatment(llm_summary)` vs `treatment(observation_mask)`. Paired by seed, per pack. Benjamini-Hochberg across the three within each pack (`results/EXP-004/analysis.json`). Each treatment against `control` and against its length-matched twin is exploratory, adjusted in its own family.

## Analysis plan
As EXP-002: Wilson intervals per cell, exact McNemar, paired bootstrap risk difference, not-applicable episodes counted and covered by the sensitivity analysis. Per cell, the share of episodes in which each fact was still in the window (`fates_kept`). Command: `ctxcheck report results/EXP-004`.

## Validity checks
Per pack: `floor` failure below about 50%, `control` failure above about 20%, or more than 20% not applicable makes that pack uninterpretable. Survival in every deterministic-policy trace must match the same policy run offline on the same seed (`scripts/survival_parity.py EXP-004`); a mismatch is a bug and nothing is reported until it is found.

## Interpretation rules (written in advance)
- A directional hypothesis is supported for a pack when the paired risk difference is in the predicted direction, its 95% bootstrap interval excludes zero and the adjusted exact McNemar q is below 0.05. In the predicted direction with an interval that includes zero: inconclusive. Opposite direction: reported as such.
- A "does not differ" prediction is reported as contradicted when the difference meets the support rule in either direction, and otherwise as not contradicted (not as "supported").
- Agreement between survival and behaviour is described per policy and fact; no pooled claim.

## What would change our mind / what would make us stop
Change: a validity failure, or the blind audit finding summaries that carry a fact in a form the verbatim check misses. Stop: free-tier caps; the achieved n is reported per cell. No optional stopping.

## Manual audit
Blind sheets (`scripts/audit_sheet.py`) for the `llm_summary` summaries, at least 20, judged per fact.

### What each policy kept, fact by fact (mechanical, from the traces)
| Policy | C1 channel rule (turn 2) | I1 account code (turn 9) | D1 deploy target (turn 16) |
|---|---|---|---|
| drop oldest | 0 of 27 | 0 of 27 | 27 of 27 |
| keep head and tail | 27 of 27 | 0 of 27 | 0 of 27 |
| mask tool output | 22 of 27 | 27 of 27 | 27 of 27 |
| model summary | 23 of 27 | 27 of 27 | 27 of 27 |

Each cell counts the episodes whose request tests that fact. The masking cell for C1 is not all seeds, against the preregistered table: see the deviations log. The request is part of the window, and the channel request (which lists the channels) is longer than the other two, so on some seeds masking alone did not fit the budget and its fallback dropped the oldest turns, the rule's among them. The offline parity run reproduces exactly those seeds.

### Verdicts (preregistered interpretation rules)
**H1** (drop oldest fails more than masking on C1 and I1, not on D1).
- C1: **supported**. Drop oldest got the channel wrong in 26 of 27; masking in 4 of 27.
- I1: **supported**. 27 of 27 against 0 of 27.
- D1: **not contradicted**: 0 of 27 against 0 of 27.

**H2** (keep head and tail fails more than masking on I1 and D1, not on C1).
- I1: **supported**. Keep head and tail got the account code wrong in 27 of 27; masking in 0 of 27.
- D1: **supported**. 19 of 26 against 0 of 27; the floor was 24 of 27, so with the target gone the agent did about as well as guessing.
- C1: **not contradicted**. Head and tail got the channel wrong in 0 of 27, masking in 4 of 27: masking did worse here, because of the fallback described above, but the adjusted q does not pass the rule.

**H3** (the summary fails at least as often as masking, on every fact). **Not contradicted** on all three: C1 4 of 27 against 4 of 27; I1 0 of 27 against 0 of 27; D1 0 of 27 against 0 of 27. Mistral's summaries carried the middle and late facts on every seed, and the early rule on most.

### Does behaviour follow survival? (exploratory)
Per policy and fact, how often the grade matched what the window held (kept and right, or gone and wrong): drop oldest on C1 26 of 27, on I1 27 of 27; head and tail on I1 27 of 27, on D1 19 of 26; masking on C1 26 of 27; summary on C1 27 of 27. Where a fact was gone and the agent was still right, it picked the right option from the request's list by chance, as the floor shows it sometimes does; an account code cannot be guessed, and never was.

### What this shows, and what it does not
- **Which policy is safe depends on where the fact sits.** Drop oldest loses early facts, keep head and tail loses the middle and late ones, and each time the agent then fails on exactly the fact that was lost. EXP-002's "keep head and tail is as good as full context" was a property of a rule planted at turn 2.
- **Masking tool output kept every fact it had room for**, and failed only where its fallback had to drop turns. That fallback is the honest cost of the policy at a tight budget.
- **The summary did well here** with Mistral, which carried the rule through in EXP-002 too. It is one model; EXP-001 and EXP-002 show that a summary's quality depends on which model writes it.
- Each seed's summary was written once and reused by the three packs (the summary request holds the history, not the final request), so within a seed the three facts were judged against the same summary.
- One model, one budget, scripted history, one compaction, synthetic facts. No claim about models or harnesses in general.
